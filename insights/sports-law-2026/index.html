Legal 500 Country Comparative Guides 2026: Ghana - Sports Law

November 11, 2026 4 min read

We are pleased to share our contribution to the 2026 Legal 500 Country Comparative Guide: Ghana - Sports Law.

Our team members, Cleopatra Nsiah Nketiah (Founding Partner and Head of Corporate Commercial) and Freda Nsarkoh (Associate), explore the evolving legal landscape of Ghana’s sports industry, examining key areas including sports governance, athlete protection, commercial rights, sponsorship, investment and dispute resolution.

As the industry continues to grow, understanding the legal and regulatory frameworks that shape it is more important than ever.

The chapter provides valuable insights into the legal considerations, challenges and opportunities shaping the future of sports in Ghana.

The Regulatory & Statutory Framework

Ghanaian sports administration operates through a dual architecture comprising national public oversight and federation-specific self-regulation. The primary legislative foundation is the Sports Act, 2016 (Act 934), as complemented by the Sports Regulations, 2023 (L.I. 2477).

Under this statutory framework, the National Sports Authority (NSA) serves as the statutory umbrella body tasked with coordinating competitive sports, registering associations, and supervising national sporting teams. Alongside statutory regulation, autonomous governing bodies, most notably the Ghana Football Association (GFA), operate under detailed statutory charters aligned with international federations (such as FIFA, CAF, and the International Olympic Committee), striking a delicate balance between domestic public law and global sporting independence.

Integrity, Anti-Doping & Athlete Safeguarding

A pivotal recent advancement in Ghanaian sports jurisprudence is the enactment of the Ghana Anti-Doping Act, 2024 (Act 1116), which formally established the Ghana Anti-Doping Agency (GADA). This legislation codifies World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) compliance into domestic law, establishing specialized testing protocols, investigatory powers, and independent adjudication panels.

Beyond anti-doping, the chapter details growing regulatory scrutiny on athlete welfare. This encompasses safeguarding policies for minors in football academies, mandatory pre-competition medical screenings, standardized employment contracts, and statutory mechanisms aimed at combating match manipulation, irregular betting, and financial misconduct.

Commercial Rights & Sponsorship Restrictions

As Ghanaian sports commercialize, rights-holders and corporate sponsors operate within an evolving intersection of contract law, intellectual property protection, and sector-specific restrictions:

  • Broadcasting & Media Rights: Collective licensing and digital transmission rights require careful structuring to navigate exclusivity covenants and competition principles.
  • Image Rights & Personality Licensing: Athletes and teams increasingly utilize bespoke licensing vehicles to protect likenesses, trademarks, and merchandise from unauthorized exploitation and ambush marketing.
  • Advertising Constraints: Direct and indirect tobacco sponsorship is strictly prohibited under the Public Health Act, 2012 (Act 851). Furthermore, Food and Drugs Authority (FDA) guidelines restrict alcohol promotions associated with youth demographics, and the Gaming Commission regulates sports betting partnerships.

Dispute Resolution & Judicial Remedies

Dispute management in Ghanaian sports prioritizes specialized alternative dispute resolution (ADR) pathways before recourse to the ordinary courts. National federations maintain structured internal judicial bodies, such as the GFA Disciplinary, Ethics, and Appeals Committees, while the NSA operates an overarching dispute committee under Regulation 32 of L.I. 2477.

For qualifying disputes of international dimension or contractual significance, federation rules provide direct appellate pathways to the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) in Lausanne, Switzerland. Ghanaian courts have consistently respected the doctrine of exhaustion of internal sporting remedies, intervening only where there are manifest breaches of natural justice or fundamental rights.

Corporate Governance & Private Investment

The chapter also addresses the commercial transformation of sporting clubs into formal corporate entities under the Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992). Modern club licensing frameworks demand transparent financial auditing, infrastructure criteria, and clear ownership structures. For international investors and private equity entrants, navigating foreign participation requirements, non-trading capital rules under the GIPC Act, and multi-club ownership boundaries represents a vital area of legal strategy as Ghanaian sports continues to modernize.
